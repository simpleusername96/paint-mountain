# Paint Mountain Gameplay Fun Redesign — Working Memo

Last updated: 2026-08-21

This is the durable working memo for the current gameplay redesign discussion.
Update this file as the conversation develops. It is advisory research and does
not override `docs/source-brief.md`, `docs/design-spec.md`, or an approved
implementation plan.

## Player experience target

- Keep each decision brief and the overall play fast.
- Require a small but meaningful decision before each shot.
- Build a tight `try -> observe -> calibrate -> retry` loop. A stage does not
  need to be cleared on the first attempt.
- Allow visible, bounded luck without making identical launches unreliable.
- Make the act of paint spreading enjoyable even before score is considered.

## Current direction from the user

- Each stage supplies a finite set of `N` ball tokens.
- The set may be generated by bounded randomness or selected from one authored
  composition for the stage. This choice remains open.
- The player may choose which unused ball to fire next. The set is a selectable
  **ball hand**, not a forced queue.
- A dedicated ball-selection UI is required. The current three-token queue is
  presentation-only and is not sufficient.
- Negative-scoring paint remains available as a design tool. It is not rejected.
- Do not hard-pair each special ball with a particular authored terrain feature.
- The cannon sees only part of the mountain. Core objectives should not require
  the player to reason accurately about hidden rear or side surfaces.

## Current implementation mismatch

- `src/ball/ball_deal_generator.gd` creates an ordered deal. For deals with at
  least four shots, the final two tokens are Standard balls of opposite colors,
  used as a correction reserve.
- `src/stage/stage_controller.gd` consumes the deal through one advancing
  `_queue_cursor`; `current_ball_token()` always returns that single next token.
- The public HUD exposes the current token and next two tokens.
- `src/ui/components/ball_queue.gd` supports hover, focus, press-to-pin details,
  and accessibility text. It does not publish a gameplay selection action.
- Free-order play therefore needs stable token identities, a remaining-token
  collection owned by `StageController`, one UI/agent-neutral select action,
  and a selectable hand component.

## Separate defect: Apex Split can hit before splitting

Current behavior in `src/projectile/apex_split_ball_behavior.gd` resolves the
split only when vertical velocity crosses the apex. A fast, low, nearly direct
shot can reach terrain first; terrain contact then permanently blocks the split.

Treat this as a separate correctness issue, not as part of the fun redesign.
The preferred rule to test is:

- Split at the earliest of the normal apex crossing or an imminent valid-terrain
  contact within a small authored lead time or lead distance.
- Keep one final contact-time fallback so a legal Apex Split never becomes a
  Standard ball merely because the arc was too flat.
- Preserve deterministic child directions and atomic three-child replacement.

No runtime fix is authorized by this memo.

## Research synthesis

The useful pattern across casual games, motor learning, sports scoring, and
visual-transformation tasks is consistent:

- Put randomness before the decision and show enough of it for planning.
- Keep the number of simultaneous choices small when decisions should stay fast.
- Keep action outcomes stable so the player can learn from the previous try.
- Report the result of a try without revealing the full solution in advance.
- Make every unit of visible transformation produce immediate layered feedback.
- Let the player stop at a satisfactory result or continue for a better one.

## Five retained design ideas

### 1. Replace the queue with a small visible ball hand

Deal about four to six balls at stage start and show all of them. The player
clicks one ball, presses its number key, and fires it next. The chosen token
moves into a clear cannon slot; fired tokens disappear and the remaining tokens
close the gap.

The composition may be seeded-random or authored, but the main prototype should
show the complete hand. Randomness creates a new puzzle; free order prevents the
run from becoming queue disposal. Offer both `Retry Same Hand` and `New Hand`.

### 2. Score only the readable front target using both fill and balance

Define the primary score surface as the broad cannon-facing region the player
can actually inspect. Rear and side paint may remain visible, but should not be
required for the core clear condition.

Clear requires both:

1. a minimum amount of newly painted front surface; and
2. the signed Red/Green score to finish inside a target band.

This keeps negative paint useful for correction. Throwing a negative ball away
also sacrifices scarce front coverage, so disposal is no longer automatically
free. Do not require every ball to be fired.

### 3. Turn every failed shot into a short calibration lesson

After a shot settles, briefly retain the first-impact marker and a simplified
ghost of the actual paint trail. Report only coarse facts such as `left`,
`right`, `short`, `long`, fresh paint gained, and score change. Keep the prior
aim and selected target when retrying.

This gives the player useful knowledge of results without predicting the full
post-impact path. `Retry Same Hand` should return to a fireable state with one
action and almost no transition.

### 4. Separate safe clearing from optional risk

As soon as coverage and the color band are valid, enable `Finish`. The player
may stop immediately or spend remaining balls to improve stars through more
fresh coverage, a score nearer the band center, or a paint-chain bonus.

Firing again means accepting the risk of leaving the band. This creates a small
push-your-luck decision after every successful correction and gives negative
paint a clear late-run purpose.

### 5. Reward continuous fresh painting as its own event

Reward new target surface painted per second, not contact with a specific
terrain mechanism. As a ball keeps finding dry surface, build a short paint
chain with stronger trail thickness, droplets, sound layers, small score pulses,
and a restrained camera pullback. Burst and Split naturally create larger peaks
without being locked to particular terrain shapes.

End each shot with a quick overview of the changed front face. Use moderate or
high layered feedback; nonstop maximum shake, particles, and sound would obscure
the route and weaken calibration.

## Coherent prototype loop

1. Receive a visible hand of roughly five balls.
2. Inspect the readable front target and choose one ball.
3. Aim and fire.
4. Watch a short, strongly presented paint result.
5. Read the compact calibration feedback.
6. Choose another ball, Finish, or instantly retry the same hand.

Initial falsification prototype: three stages, one five-ball hand size, free
order, seeded Same/New Hand retries, front-only objective, total-coverage floor,
signed color band, and the fresh-paint chain. Do not migrate all thirty stages
until this loop is enjoyable in repeated manual play.

## Evaluation questions

- Does the player choose different first balls for understandable reasons?
- Is average pre-shot decision time short without becoming automatic?
- Does a failed shot make the next adjustment clearer?
- Does the player ever dump a ball outside the objective, and is that actually
  beneficial?
- Does `Finish or continue` create a real decision?
- Is watching a long fresh-paint route enjoyable without reading the score UI?
- Does Same Hand retry lead to visible improvement within two or three attempts?
