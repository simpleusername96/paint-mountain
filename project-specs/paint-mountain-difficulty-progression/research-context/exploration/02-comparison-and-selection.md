# Comparison and Selection

## Purpose

Compare the frozen candidates without false numeric precision and choose one.

## Sources

- `01-candidate-ledger.md`
- `../research/01-local-product-truth.md`

## Findings

| Candidate | Immediate comprehension | Pre-launch planning | Core fit | Implementation risk | Fairness/readability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N1 Resident Ball Bumpers | High | High | Medium | Very high | Medium-low |
| N2 Two-Shot Relay | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium-low |
| N3 Shared Propellant | Very high | Very high | Very high | Low | High |
| N4 Aim-Axis Lock | High | High | Low | Low | Low |
| N5 Target Archipelago | Very high | High | High | Medium | High |

N1 creates the most emergent play but conflicts with current collision behavior
and deterministic readability. N2 can use current root concurrency, yet its
difficulty is partly input timing. N4 is easy to build but makes experimentation
punitive. N5 is the strongest fallback, though content and target readability
costs are larger and repeated region coverage can feel procedural.

N3 is the only candidate that is simultaneously one-sentence clear, strongly
strategic before firing, native to the existing power control, and low-risk to
physics. Its feasibility risk is explicit and reversible: raise the affected
authored budget when evidence fails.

## Recommendations

Select **N3 Shared Propellant** and use N5 only as a fallback after a documented
playtest failure that budget tuning cannot resolve.

## Limitations

- The table is a comparative judgment, not measured player data.
