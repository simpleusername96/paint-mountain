## Findings

- The current product already increases terrain dimensions, route count,
  reversals, ridges, basins, passes, undulation, mechanism count, target
  coverage, shots, and duration across thirty all-open stages.
- The player retains the same high-level loop: inspect, choose a target and
  launch parameters, fire, observe contact and paint, revise, then Finish or
  reach the time limit.
- The weighted scalar difficulty value is used only by a monotonic progression
  test; runtime, UI, observations, and saves do not consume it.
- Direct precedents commonly introduce a mechanic and then combine it, tighten
  spatial/resource constraints, add optional mastery, and preserve readable
  feedback. They do not directly solve Paint Mountain's continuous-coverage,
  committed-launch route problem.
- Education, sports practice, aviation training, and variation theory converge
  on an authored sequence: establish an invariant, vary one aspect, contrast,
  transfer, interleave, and fuse. Their evidence concerns learning, not fun.
- Golf and orienteering contribute an in-stage structure: visible safe and
  high-challenge route options create strategic choice without relying only on
  distance or hidden difficulty.
- Adaptive assessment is not currently supportable because partial coverage is
  not a calibrated right/wrong response and no persistent skill model exists.
- Twelve candidates were frozen before scoring. The selected C12 combines the
  contrastive sequence with readable risk-route choices and requires no new
  runtime rule.

## Limitations

- Existing automated checks prove contracts, not perceived difficulty or fun.
- External learning and safety evidence transfers causal structures only.
- No player study or live balance telemetry has been performed for this task.
- Final numeric route tuning remains subject to deterministic generation and
  representative running-game playtests.
